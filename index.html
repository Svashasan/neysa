Svashasan Labs

Building and Commercialising Svashasan OS Through an AI Infrastructure Partnership

Purpose: Establish an 18–24-month partnership in which Neysa AI acts as preferred AI infrastructure and compute partner for Svashasan OS, giving Neysa a slightly greater near-term commercial upside, while Svashasan Labs retains ownership of Svashasan OS and its core intellectual property.

Document type: Proposed strategic partnership framework
Proposed duration: 18–24 months
Parties: Neysa AI and Svashasan Labs
Status: Discussion proposal — all commercial terms and commitments are subject to mutual agreement

1. Executive Summary

Svashasan Labs proposes an 18–24-month strategic partnership with Neysa AI to accelerate the development, validation, demonstration and eventual commercial deployment of Svashasan OS, Svashasan Labs' operating-system-level software platform for electric vehicles and autonomous-driving capabilities.

Under the proposed arrangement, Neysa AI would act as the preferred AI infrastructure and compute partner for eligible Svashasan OS development workloads. Neysa could provide initial GPU compute credits or subsidised infrastructure, engineering assistance, workload optimisation and preferential commercial pricing. Svashasan Labs would contribute its automotive software architecture, autonomy stack, integration work, validation requirements and OEM engagement.

The collaboration is designed to be mutually beneficial while giving Neysa a slightly greater near-term commercial upside: Neysa receives a pathway to recurring infrastructure consumption, a specialised automotive AI use case, and potential joint enterprise opportunities. Svashasan receives lower initial infrastructure costs, access to scalable compute and support in progressing towards OEM demonstrations and pilots.

The partnership should begin with a measurable proof of concept and expand only when technical and commercial milestones are met. Svashasan Labs should retain ownership of its existing and newly developed Svashasan OS intellectual property, product roadmap and OEM relationships. The initial proposal does not require equity investment or unconditional exclusivity.

2. Strategic Objective

The shared objective is to establish a credible development and deployment pathway for Svashasan OS by combining:

  • Neysa AI's contribution: AI compute infrastructure, GPU capacity, infrastructure expertise, workload orchestration and scalable AI deployment support.
  • Svashasan Labs' contribution: Automotive OS architecture, perception and prediction components, sensor-fusion integration, decision-making and vehicle-control software, simulation workflows, validation requirements and OEM-facing product development.
  • Joint outcome: A technically demonstrated automotive software platform supported by an infrastructure partner, with a pathway towards OEM evaluation, pilot projects and recurring infrastructure consumption.
Cloud compute alone does not make an automotive system production-ready.

Real-time in-vehicle inference, target-hardware performance, functional safety, cybersecurity and regulatory validation must be assessed separately against the intended deployment environment.

3. Proposed Partnership Model

3.1 Neysa AI: Preferred AI Infrastructure Partner

Neysa's proposed responsibilities would include:

1. GPU compute access

  • Offer a defined amount of GPU compute credits, a subsidised pilot environment or discounted access to suitable infrastructure, subject to internal approval.
  • Support selected model-training, experimentation, simulation, evaluation and data-processing workloads.
  • Agree on usage limits, availability, expiry dates, service levels and any overage charges before work begins.

2. Infrastructure setup and optimisation

  • Help configure the development environment and relevant compute resources.
  • Advise on workload scheduling, resource utilisation, orchestration, monitoring and cost optimisation.
  • Help identify which workloads benefit from GPUs and which can run more economically on CPU or other suitable infrastructure.

3. Engineering collaboration

  • Provide reasonable technical assistance during setup and the agreed proof of concept.
  • Work with Svashasan to define workload benchmarks, infrastructure requirements and cost estimates.
  • Help review scaling options as the project moves from experimentation to larger workloads.

4. Deployment and scaling support

  • Advise on repeatable environments for model development and evaluation.
  • Support scaling eligible cloud-based workloads as development and customer activity expands.
  • Define operational, security, availability and support expectations for any paid production service.

5. Commercial and go-to-market participation

  • Participate in agreed joint demonstrations and approved case studies.
  • Use the collaboration as a potential reference for serving specialised automotive and mobility AI workloads.
  • Pursue relevant customer opportunities jointly where both parties agree that their offerings are complementary.

3.2 Svashasan Labs: Automotive OS and Product Partner

Svashasan Labs' proposed responsibilities would include:

1. Product architecture and development

  • Continue developing and integrating Svashasan OS.
  • Own the automotive software architecture and product roadmap.
  • Integrate and improve relevant components such as perception, prediction, sensor fusion, decision-making, navigation interfaces and vehicle-control interfaces, as applicable to the agreed pilot scope.

2. Workload definition

  • Identify which development and testing workloads require accelerated compute.
  • Provide workload profiles, data requirements, expected throughput and measurable evaluation criteria.
  • Separate cloud development workloads from latency-sensitive workloads intended to execute on in-vehicle hardware.

3. Technical evaluation

  • Run agreed workloads and report results against jointly defined benchmarks.
  • Evaluate cost, performance, reliability and reproducibility.
  • Document limitations, dependencies and outstanding engineering work.

4. OEM and automotive engagement

  • Lead automotive product positioning, OEM outreach and relevant Tier-1 supplier discussions.
  • Identify potential pilot requirements and communicate them to Neysa where appropriate and authorised.
  • Obtain permission before sharing confidential OEM information or customer data.

5. Commercial transition

  • Prioritise Neysa for eligible workloads when Neysa meets the agreed requirements for performance, security, service levels and pricing.
  • Move to paid usage only after agreed pilot milestones, budget approval and written commercial terms.
  • Provide feedback and, where mutually approved, contribute to joint demonstrations and case studies.

4. Joint Technical Workstream

The work should be divided into clearly scoped workstreams rather than treating all of Svashasan OS as one undifferentiated cloud workload.

Workstream A: Compute and Development Environment

Neysa AI

  • Propose suitable infrastructure options and an initial cost model.
  • Help configure access, resource limits, monitoring and workload isolation.
  • Recommend an approach for tracking usage and managing cost.

Svashasan Labs

  • Supply the workload requirements and development dependencies.
  • Define access controls and the minimum data necessary for the pilot.
  • Validate that the environment supports the intended development workflow.

Deliverables

  • Agreed infrastructure architecture.
  • Initial usage and cost estimate.
  • Environment setup checklist.
  • Access-control and operational responsibilities.

Workstream B: Model Development and Evaluation

Neysa AI

  • Provide agreed compute capacity for selected training, experimentation and evaluation workloads.
  • Help optimise resource allocation and execution efficiency.

Svashasan Labs

  • Select the models and workloads included in the pilot.
  • Define dataset handling, evaluation methods and benchmark criteria.
  • Analyse model quality, latency and resource consumption.

Deliverables

  • Reproducible workload runs.
  • Model evaluation report.
  • Compute utilisation and cost report.
  • List of bottlenecks and proposed improvements.

Workstream C: Simulation and System Integration

Neysa AI

  • Support infrastructure needs for eligible simulation, batch processing and analysis workloads.
  • Advise on scalable execution where workloads can be parallelised.

Svashasan Labs

  • Integrate the relevant software components with the selected simulator and ROS 2-based workflow, where applicable.
  • Define scenario coverage and system-level test criteria.
  • Verify the software behaviour and record known limitations.

Deliverables

  • Demonstration build for the agreed scope.
  • Scenario and test report.
  • System integration findings.
  • Clear separation of simulation results from real-world validation claims.

Workstream D: OEM Demonstration and Pilot Preparation

Neysa AI

  • Provide agreed infrastructure support for demonstration workloads.
  • Participate in the demonstration where relevant and mutually approved.
  • Supply accurate information about the infrastructure used.

Svashasan Labs

  • Own the automotive product demonstration and technical narrative.
  • Prepare the product architecture, evaluation results and pilot plan.
  • Manage OEM engagement and ensure that claims match the evidence available.

Deliverables

  • Demonstration package.
  • Technical architecture overview.
  • Benchmark and limitation summary.
  • Proposed OEM pilot scope and resource requirements.

5. Commercial Structure: Giving Neysa a Slightly Greater Benefit

The commercial design should give Neysa a clearer path to near-term and recurring infrastructure revenue, while providing Svashasan Labs with the support required to progress towards a credible OEM demonstration.

5.1 Phase One: Initial Infrastructure Support

  • Request an initial 3–6-month support window through GPU credits, subsidised infrastructure or a discounted pilot environment.
  • Agree on a defined workload, resource cap, support scope and expiry date.
  • Use the initial phase to establish whether Neysa's infrastructure is technically and commercially suitable.
  • Avoid open-ended usage or ambiguous commitments to pay for excess consumption.

Benefit to Neysa: Early engagement with a specialised automotive AI workload and the opportunity to demonstrate its infrastructure capabilities.

Benefit to Svashasan: Reduced upfront compute expenditure and a faster route to completing the agreed development and evaluation work.

5.2 Phase Two: Preferred-Provider Arrangement

Svashasan would prioritise Neysa for eligible workloads, provided Neysa satisfies mutually agreed requirements for:

  • Performance and availability.
  • Security and access controls.
  • Technical support.
  • Commercial pricing.
  • Data handling and contractual requirements.

A proposed negotiation target is 30–40% preferential pricing against an agreed comparable baseline, subject to Neysa's approval and a clear definition of the baseline, eligible services, duration and exclusions.

Preferred-provider status should not amount to unconditional exclusivity. Svashasan should retain the ability to use alternatives if Neysa cannot meet agreed requirements or if a workload has specific technical, regulatory, customer or cost constraints.

5.3 Phase Three: Paid Infrastructure Consumption

After successful completion of the proof of concept and approval of the next-stage budget:

  • Svashasan transitions relevant workloads to paid Neysa infrastructure.
  • Both parties agree on a forecast based on actual workload needs rather than speculative future volumes.
  • Any minimum spend, committed-use discount or reservation is documented separately.
  • Consumption commitments increase only as workloads, funding and customer activity justify them.

Benefit to Neysa: Potential recurring GPU and platform revenue as Svashasan's development and deployment needs grow.

Benefit to Svashasan: Continued access to infrastructure with clearer cost planning and the ability to scale as needed.

5.4 Phase Four: Joint Go-to-Market

The parties may jointly pursue:

  • Automotive and mobility AI demonstrations.
  • Approved technical case studies.
  • Relevant OEM, Tier-1 supplier or enterprise opportunities.
  • Infrastructure opportunities connected to model training, simulation, evaluation or fleet-data processing.

Each opportunity should have a written agreement defining lead ownership, customer communications, pricing, delivery responsibilities, revenue allocation if applicable, confidentiality and use of each party's name or logo.

Neither party should represent that an OEM has committed to purchase, pilot or deploy Svashasan OS unless that commitment has been expressly documented by the OEM.

7. Intellectual Property, Data and Security Protections

These terms should be addressed before confidential technical information or production-relevant data is shared.

7.1 Intellectual Property

  • Svashasan Labs retains ownership of its pre-existing intellectual property, including Svashasan OS architecture, automotive software, algorithms, source code, designs, documentation and related know-how.
  • Svashasan Labs should also retain ownership of newly developed Svashasan OS components created independently by its team, subject to any separately negotiated written agreement.
  • Neysa retains ownership of its pre-existing infrastructure technology, platforms, tools, methods and documentation.
  • Neither party receives an implied licence to the other's intellectual property beyond the limited rights required to perform the agreed work.
  • Any jointly developed component, modification, reusable tooling or invention must have ownership and licensing terms explicitly documented before development begins.
  • Access to Svashasan's source code should be limited to what is necessary for the agreed scope.

7.2 Data and Model Assets

The agreement should define:

  • Ownership and permitted use of datasets.
  • Access to raw data, derived data, logs and telemetry.
  • Ownership and handling of model weights, checkpoints, evaluation outputs and fine-tuned assets.
  • Whether any data or model artefacts may be retained after the pilot.
  • Retention periods, deletion procedures and audit rights.
  • Restrictions on using confidential data or model artefacts to train or improve unrelated services.

Customer data should not be shared without appropriate authorisation and contractual safeguards.

7.3 Security and Confidentiality

  • Use role-based access controls and least-privilege permissions.
  • Separate development, testing and production environments where appropriate.
  • Agree on encryption, secrets management, logging and incident-notification procedures.
  • Use a mutual NDA and a written data-processing arrangement where applicable.
  • Document security responsibilities for infrastructure and application layers.
  • Define how access is revoked and data is returned or deleted when the engagement ends.

7.4 Automotive Safety and Deployment Boundaries

The collaboration should distinguish between cloud-based model development and real-time vehicle operation.

  • Cloud compute results do not establish that an in-vehicle system meets its real-time performance requirements.
  • Production use requires validation on the intended automotive compute hardware and target software configuration.
  • Functional safety, cybersecurity, software update security and relevant regulatory requirements remain subject to their own engineering and compliance processes.
  • Simulation benchmarks must not be presented as proof of road safety or production readiness without supporting evidence.

8. Governance and Operating Model

Establish a lightweight joint working group with one technical and one commercial point of contact from each company.

Proposed governance cadence

  • Weekly during the proof of concept: Technical progress, blockers, compute usage and next steps.
  • Monthly: Milestone review, costs, risks and resource requirements.
  • At each phase gate: Written acceptance or a documented decision to revise, pause or stop the work.

9. Proposed Roadmap: 18–24 Months

The following roadmap is a planning proposal, not a guaranteed delivery schedule.

Month 1 — Partnership Design

  • Align on business objectives and the initial workload.
  • Estimate compute requirements and cost.
  • Agree on IP, confidentiality, data handling, support and access controls.
  • Define success criteria and the phase-one budget.
  • Sign the required NDA and pilot or evaluation agreement.

Exit criteria: Both parties approve the scope, resources, owners and measurement plan.

Months 2–3 — Technical Proof of Concept

  • Configure the development environment.
  • Run selected training, simulation and evaluation workloads.
  • Measure model results, resource utilisation, cost and execution performance.
  • Document issues and optimisation opportunities.

Exit criteria: The agreed workloads run successfully and the results are documented against the agreed criteria.

Months 4–6 — Demonstration Preparation

  • Improve the selected components based on evaluation findings.
  • Package a stable demonstration build.
  • Prepare a technical architecture overview and evidence-based benchmark report.
  • Identify the hardware and validation work still required for an OEM pilot.

Exit criteria: A repeatable demonstration is ready for the agreed scope, with known limitations clearly disclosed.

Months 6–12 — OEM Evaluation and Commercial Expansion

  • Pursue suitable OEM and Tier-1 evaluations.
  • Define pilot requirements with interested counterparties.
  • Move relevant workloads to paid infrastructure when budget and commercial terms are approved.
  • Explore joint case studies and customer opportunities where permitted.

Exit criteria: Progress is measured through documented customer evaluations, agreed pilot scopes and actual workload demand — not assumed purchase commitments.

Months 12–24 — Scale and Optimise

  • Expand infrastructure consumption only when justified by workload and customer needs.
  • Optimise performance and cost based on observed usage.
  • Establish repeatable deployment and monitoring processes.
  • Review whether the preferred-provider arrangement remains commercially and technically appropriate.

Exit criteria: A sustainable infrastructure relationship supported by measurable workloads, agreed service levels and a credible product deployment pathway.

10. Success Metrics

The parties should agree on numerical targets after the workload and baseline have been assessed. Suggested measurement categories include:

Technical metrics

  • Percentage of agreed workloads completed successfully.
  • Model evaluation results against the chosen baseline.
  • Training and evaluation runtime.
  • Compute utilisation and cost per workload.
  • Reproducibility of results.
  • Infrastructure availability and incident response against agreed service levels.

Product and validation metrics

  • Completion of the defined Svashasan OS demonstration scope.
  • Number of test scenarios completed and documented.
  • Performance measured on the intended target hardware where relevant.
  • Open defects, known limitations and outstanding safety or security work.
  • Readiness of the technical evidence package for an OEM evaluation.

Commercial metrics

  • Initial infrastructure support delivered as agreed.
  • Cost savings against a clearly defined baseline.
  • Paid infrastructure consumption after the pilot.
  • Qualified joint opportunities and documented next steps.
  • Customer evaluations or pilot agreements actually secured.
  • Renewal and expansion decisions based on real usage.
Do not set or advertise performance figures before they have been measured and verified.

12. Proposed Negotiation Priorities

Priority 1 — Secure a defined pilot

Ask Neysa to help scope a limited initial engagement with clear infrastructure resources, engineering support, usage limits and measurable outcomes.

Priority 2 — Obtain initial compute support

Request a 3–6-month credits or subsidised infrastructure arrangement. Treat this as a negotiation request, not an assumed entitlement.

Priority 3 — Establish preferential commercial terms

Discuss a target of 30–40% preferential pricing against a mutually agreed baseline for eligible workloads, subject to service scope and Neysa's approval.

Priority 4 — Create a path to recurring revenue for Neysa

Offer to prioritise Neysa for qualifying workloads and transition to paid usage after successful milestones, without committing to unsupported minimum spend.

Priority 5 — Protect Svashasan's core assets

Retain OS ownership, control over the product roadmap and OEM relationships, and the ability to use alternative providers when necessary.

Priority 6 — Build a joint market reference

After results are validated, pursue an approved demonstration or case study showing how Neysa's infrastructure supports the relevant development workloads for an automotive software platform.

13. Suggested Outreach Message to Neysa's CTO

I'd like to explore a strategic partnership between Svashasan Labs and Neysa AI, where Neysa powers the scalable compute infrastructure behind Svashasan OS while gaining a long-term, high-growth autonomous mobility workload, recurring infrastructure revenue and joint enterprise opportunities as we move towards OEM pilots.

The first meeting should focus on Neysa's interest in the workload, the infrastructure required, the support it could offer, and the commercial structure that would make a pilot worthwhile for both parties.

14. Bottom Line of the Deal

Svashasan Labs proposes an 18–24-month strategic partnership with Neysa AI to accelerate the development and commercial deployment of Svashasan OS, with Neysa serving as its preferred AI infrastructure partner. Neysa would support the collaboration through initial GPU compute credits or subsidised infrastructure, engineering assistance and preferential commercial pricing for eligible workloads, enabling Svashasan to advance model development, simulation, validation and OEM demonstrations with lower upfront capital expenditure. In return, Svashasan would prioritise Neysa for qualifying workloads, transition to paid infrastructure consumption after successful pilot milestones, and collaborate on joint demonstrations, case studies and relevant enterprise opportunities. The arrangement gives Neysa a potential recurring infrastructure revenue stream and a differentiated automotive AI use case, while Svashasan retains ownership of its OS intellectual property, product roadmap and OEM relationships. The partnership would begin with a measurable proof of concept and expand commercially based on demonstrated results, without requiring equity or unconditional exclusivity.

15. Important Status Note

This document is a proposed collaboration strategy for discussion. It does not state that Neysa AI has agreed to provide credits, discounts, engineering resources, customer introductions or any other benefit. All commercial figures, timelines, responsibilities, service levels and legal terms must be reviewed and agreed by both parties in writing before being treated as commitments.
